How a Stock Market Works

A stock market is a place where buyers and sellers trade shares of companies. When a company wants to raise money, it can go public, which means it sells part of its ownership to the public. People who buy those shares become shareholders.

Share prices change every day. They move according to supply and demand: if more people want to buy a share than sell it, the price goes up. News about profits, interest rates or the economy can also affect prices. Periods of rising prices are called a bull market, and periods of falling prices are called a bear market.

Some companies pay part of their profits to shareholders. This payment is called a dividend. Not every company pays one. To follow the market as a whole, analysts use an index, such as the S&P 500, which tracks the prices of a group of large companies.

All investments involve risk, and prices can fall as well as rise. For this reason, professionals use the word diversification to describe holding different types of assets in one portfolio.
